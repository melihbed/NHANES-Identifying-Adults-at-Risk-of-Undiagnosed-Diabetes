Undiagnosed Diabetes Screening — interview walkthrough

30-second pitch: "Using public NHANES survey data, I built a calibrated model that ranks adults who've never been diagnosed with diabetes by their probability of already having a diabetes-range HbA1c. As a triage filter it flags the highest-risk third of adults and catches 22 of 23 undiagnosed cases in the held-out test — a proof of concept for deciding whom to send for a confirmatory lab test, packaged so it re-runs with one command."

01 Talk track — ~10–12 min

  1. The problem ~1 min
    ~1 in 5 people with diabetes are undiagnosed; damage accumulates silently. A cheap confirmatory test (HbA1c) exists — the real question is whom to test. That's a data-science problem.
  2. Question & data ~1 min
    NHANES Aug 2021–23. Four modules linked on the respondent id: demographics, the prior-diagnosis question, HbA1c, body measurements.
  3. Cohort ~1.5 min
    11,933 → 4,698: adults 20+, self-reported no prior diagnosis, HbA1c measured, not pregnant. Call out the 20-not-18 decision (education variable) and the HbA1c-only outcome scope (weighted 2.0% vs CDC 4.5%).
  4. The core challenge ~1 min
    2.5% positive. "Predict everyone negative" = 97.5% accuracy and useless. So: PR-AUC, recall, calibration — never accuracy. Class weights, not resampling.
  5. EDA ~1.5 min
    Risk climbs with waist, age, disadvantage. Waist ≥110 cm carries 16× the rate of <90 cm. Sex: no association. BMI–waist ρ = 0.89.
  6. Modeling decisions ~2.5 min
    Drop BMI keep waist (collinearity → sign flip). Logistic beats RF/HistGB (trees collapse to the majority class on 93 positives) and gives odds ratios. Tuning surface flat → parsimony rule.
  7. Calibration + threshold ~1.5 min
    Platt scaling → Brier 0.024. Threshold picked on training out-of-fold scores for 80% recall; test set scored exactly once.
  8. Results ~1.5 min
    Held-out: recall 0.96 (22/23), specificity 0.69, ROC-AUC 0.84, PR-AUC ≈ 3× no-skill. Frame as triage: 13 false alarms per true case, each resolved by a lab test.
  9. Honesty ~1 min
    23 test positives → wide interval. Observational. Demographic features need an equity review. "Proof of concept with an honest error bar, not a device."
  10. Engineering + next ~1 min
    make all, 11 tests, packaged src/, model card, BI extract pack. Next: v2 features (BP/smoking/activity), external validation on 2017–20, decision-curve analysis.

04 Hard questions & answers

Precision is 7% — isn't this model useless?

It's a first-stage screen; every flag goes to a confirmatory HbA1c anyway. PR-AUC is 3× the no-skill rate and the model is calibrated, so I can re-set the threshold to whatever recall/workload the clinic can absorb — without retraining.

Only 23 test positives — how much can you trust that recall?

You can't, and I say so on the slide. The point estimate is 0.96 but the interval is wide. This is a proof of concept; the next step is external validation on the 2017–March 2020 NHANES cycle.

Why not XGBoost / a neural net?

6 features, 93 positives, a near-linear signal. I tried RF and HistGB — both collapsed to the majority class. Added complexity here is risk, not value, and I'd lose the odds ratios a clinician can sanity-check.

Is it ethical to use race/ethnicity and education as features?

They act as proxies for social determinants and access to care. The model card flags this for an equity review, and I'd evaluate a body-measurement-only variant as an alternative before anything went near deployment.

How would you put this in production?

src.pipeline already produces a versioned, calibrated model artifact + metrics. Add: threshold monitoring, calibration-drift tracking against incoming HbA1c results, a decision-curve analysis to set the threshold on an explicit cost basis, and survey-weighted inference for design-correct intervals.

Your weighted prevalence (2.0%) doesn't match CDC's 4.5%. Bug?

No — deliberate scope. CDC's undiagnosed-diabetes definition is HbA1c ≥ 6.5% or fasting glucose ≥ 126. I used HbA1c only to avoid the separate fasting-subsample weight. It under-counts true cases, and that's stated in the limitations.

Why one train/test split instead of nested CV for the final estimate?

Model selection and tuning all happen inside CV on the training set; the held-out set is only for the final unbiased read. With 23 positives, nested CV folds would each have ~4–5 positives — too noisy to be more honest than a single held-out set. I report the CV spread alongside.

Volunteer these before you're asked

  • The recall estimate is fragile — 23 test positives.
  • HbA1c-only outcome under-counts true undiagnosed diabetes.
  • Observational, cross-sectional — associations, not causal effects.
  • Demographic features carry equity risk; a measurement-only variant is worth testing.
  • Survey design isn't folded into the model's standard errors yet.

Naming your own limitations first reads as seniority, not weakness.
